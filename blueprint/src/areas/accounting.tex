\begin{theorem}[Billing after delivery (C-22)]
  \label{thm:c22-bill-after-delivery}
  \uses{def:c08-delivery-status}
  A service provider does not bill the airline for a service before it has
  been delivered or consumed, unless the two have agreed otherwise.

  Source: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Settlement value of a service (C-142)]
  \label{def:accounting-settlement-value}
  \uses{def:order, def:c01-structure, def:money}
  \notready
  Each service in an order carries a settlement value for the provider that
  delivers it: the amount that provider may bill the ORA. The order holds
  this value from the moment it is created or changed, and financial
  processes read it from the order without recalculating it.

  Case to check: A partner's agreed settlement value of 120.00 for a segment
  sold inside a 100.00 bundled item makes the ORA pay out more than it
  collected; no source forbids this.

  Open point: Resolution 797 uses the term without defining it. The business
  must decide whether the value is held per service or per order item,
  whether it includes taxes and fees, and whether a provider's settlement
  values may exceed the price of the item they sit in.

  Sources: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/617185338}{Order Accounting, De-specializing Financial Processes};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/617185366}{Order Accounting, Principles for Finance with Offer and Order}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Billing limited to delivered services (C-143)]
  \label{thm:accounting-billed-within-delivered}
  \uses{thm:c22-bill-after-delivery, def:accounting-settlement-value, def:c08-delivery-status}
  \notready
  At any time, the total a service provider has billed the ORA for an order
  is at most the sum of its settlement values for services in that order
  that have been delivered or consumed, unless a bilateral agreement allows
  earlier billing. The bound covers what the ORA pays providers; the
  seller's payment to the ORA is cleared within a day of payment and lies
  outside it.

  Case to check: A seat whose passenger did not show ends \texttt{NOT
  CLAIMED}. If that counts as consumed, the provider bills for a seat nobody
  used; if it does not, the provider can never bill for a seat it held.

  Open point: The resolution says delivered and/or consumed without mapping
  the words to delivery statuses. The business must decide whether
  \texttt{NOT CLAIMED} (a no-show), \texttt{EXPIRED}, or a delivered service
  that is later refunded counts, and how a bilateral exception is recorded.

  Sources: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/617185366}{Order Accounting, Principles for Finance with Offer and Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574653069}{Settlement with Orders, Clearance Overview}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[First accounting posting after payment (C-144)]
  \label{thm:accounting-accounting-after-payment}
  \uses{def:payment, def:c08-delivery-status, def:c11-payment-time-limit, def:c12-zero-commitment}
  \notready
  The airline's accounting system receives nothing about an order until at
  least one of its items has a successful payment. The first posting records
  a liability for the services' values, including taxes and fees, and a
  receivable for the amount paid.

  Case to check: An order has two items, one paid and one still waiting for
  payment. If the first posting books liability for both, accounting shows
  an obligation for a service nobody has paid for.

  Open point: The source does not say whether that liability covers only the
  paid items or every item in the order. The business must decide, and must
  say whether a commitment to pay zero (C-12) counts as a successful payment
  here.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/578912546}{Order Accounting, Order Accounting Home};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/578813954}{Order Accounting, New Order Creation};

  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/617185366}{Order Accounting, Principles for Finance with Offer and Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/579010561}{Order Accounting, Introduction};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};

\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Revenue recognized at final status (C-145)]
  \label{thm:accounting-revenue-at-final-status}
  \uses{thm:accounting-accounting-after-payment, def:c08-delivery-status, def:c05-holds-value, thm:c22-bill-after-delivery}
  \notready
  Revenue for a paid service is recognized only after the airline reports to
  accounting that the service has reached a final delivery status. Until
  then the amount paid for it stays a liability.

  Case to check: A cancellation with a 25.00 penalty moves every service to
  \texttt{CANCELLED} and \texttt{REMOVED}. No service is delivered, so a
  rule keyed to delivery never recognizes the penalty.

  Open point: The sources tie recognition to final delivery status but do
  not say which final statuses earn revenue. The business must decide, for
  \texttt{DELIVERED}, \texttt{NOT CLAIMED}, \texttt{EXPIRED} and
  \texttt{REMOVED}, whether each recognizes revenue, releases the liability
  as a refund, or keeps it, and when penalties and forfeited amounts
  reported at cancellation count as revenue.

  Sources: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ACCSTS/}{IATA capability ACCSTS};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/579272705}{Order Accounting, Service Consumption};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844333143}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a cancellation to paid Order or OrderItems resulting in Partial Amount Refunded with a Forfeited amount and a penalty with reporting to Accounting};
  \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652427/Messages}{EASD guide, Messages};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/617185391}{Order Accounting, Accounting messages}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Money balance of an order (C-146)]
  \label{thm:accounting-order-money-balance}
  \uses{def:totals, thm:refunds-le-payments, thm:accounting-revenue-at-final-status, thm:accounting-accounting-after-payment, def:c05-holds-value, thm:c16-penalty-net}
  \notready
  For each order, the total of successful payments equals the total refunded
  plus the paid value still held as liability plus revenue recognized plus
  forfeited amounts. With every term at zero or above, this yields the
  result that refunds never exceed payments.

  Case to check: A customer pays 100.00, cancels, and receives 90.00 because
  a 10.00 tax is non-refundable. Without a forfeited term the 10.00 is
  neither refunded, held nor earned, and the balance fails.

  Open point: The sources send payments, refunds as negative payments,
  penalties and forfeited amounts to accounting but never state a balance.
  The business must decide whether forfeited non-refundable taxes form their
  own term and whether a value store counts as held liability.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844333086}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a cancellation to paid Order or OrderItems};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844333143}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a cancellation to paid Order or OrderItems resulting in Partial Amount Refunded with a Forfeited amount and a penalty with reporting to Accounting};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844169268}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a cancellation to paid Order or OrderItems resulting in Value Store};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/617185366}{Order Accounting, Principles for Finance with Offer and Order}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Commission on an item (C-147)]
  \label{def:accounting-commission-structure}
  \uses{def:money, def:offer-item}
  \notready
  A commission on an item is either a fixed amount or a percentage of a
  stated base amount, and never both. An item may carry several commissions
  with different codes, such as a standard, a supplemental and an SwO
  clearance incentive, and the seller is owed their total.

  Case to check: In the guide's Italian VAT example the commission of 4.50
  already includes 0.41 of tax. A seller reading 4.50 as net expects 4.95;
  one reading it as gross expects 4.50.

  Open point: The guide says several commissions would be applied without
  saying they add. The business must decide how they combine, whether the
  percentage base is the fare alone or includes taxes and fees, how a
  percentage result is rounded, and whether a commission subject to
  consumption tax is stated gross or net of that tax.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849050}{EASD guide, Commission and Margin};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/2588377182}{EASD guide, Consumption Taxes};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574456090}{Settlement with Orders, Codeset List};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/PAYCOM/}{IATA capability PAYCOM}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Net clearance amount (C-148)]
  \label{def:accounting-net-clearance-amount}
  \uses{def:payment, def:refund, def:accounting-commission-structure}
  \notready
  For each payment commitment, the net clearance amount is the money that
  moves between the ORA and the seller, with one of them as payer. When the
  seller collected the customer's money it is the payment amount minus the
  seller's commission and the seller pays; when the airline collected it, it
  is the commission and the airline pays. For a refund through the seller it
  is the refund minus the commission first paid on it, and the airline pays.

  Case to check: The source's refund example writes 7.50 minus 75.00, gets
  minus 67.50, and labels it a net settlement amount. Taken literally, the
  seller pays the airline for the customer's refund.

  Open point: The standard defines the amount only as what the two parties
  agreed; the formulas come from worked examples. The business must decide
  whether the formula is fixed or open to agreement, and how commission is
  recalled on a refund, since the source recalls it only where the market
  allows and gives no rule for partial refunds.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574915056}{Settlement with Orders, Definitions};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574653091}{Settlement with Orders, Enhanced and Simplified Distribution};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574259760}{Settlement with Orders, Request for Clearance};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574816478}{Settlement with Orders, Settlement};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574881933}{Settlement with Orders, Process Overview};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574816478/Settlement}{Settlement with Orders, Settlement}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Commission cleared exactly once (C-149)]
  \label{thm:accounting-commission-cleared-once}
  \uses{def:accounting-net-clearance-amount, def:accounting-commission-structure, def:payment}
  For each payment, the seller's commission is cleared exactly once: either
  netted inside the payment's net clearance amount or in a separate
  clearance in which the airline pays. Either way, the airline's net receipt
  for the payment is the payment amount minus the commission.

  Case to check: An EasyPay payment of 410.00 is authorized and cleared net
  at 369.00 after a 41.00 commission. If the airline also sends a separate
  41.00 commission clearance, the seller receives the commission twice.

  Sources: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/PAYCMT/}{IATA capability PAYCMT};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574259760}{Settlement with Orders, Request for Clearance}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Clearance request matches the order (C-150)]
  \label{thm:accounting-clearance-matches-order}
  \uses{def:accounting-net-clearance-amount, def:payment, thm:c21-version}
  The amount, currency, payer, payee and payment method in a clearance
  request equal the clearance data agreed in the order at payment
  commitment. The seller proposes its role and the amount, the ORA adds a
  clearance ID and the commitment time only if it agrees, and the order
  keeps that ID permanently.

  Case to check: With deferred EasyPay payment the airline quotes an
  expected net clearance amount at order creation. If the seller adds a bag
  before paying and reuses the old figure, the clearance disagrees with the
  order unless the ORA checks it again at payment.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574259760}{Settlement with Orders, Request for Clearance};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574881945}{Settlement with Orders, Clearance Information During Shopping};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574653091}{Settlement with Orders, Enhanced and Simplified Distribution}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[One clearance per payment commitment (C-151)]
  \label{thm:accounting-clearance-per-commitment}
  \uses{def:c13-several-methods, def:payment, thm:accounting-clearance-matches-order}
  \notready
  Every clearance comes from one payment commitment, identified by the order
  ID and the payment ID, and each commitment has at most one accepted
  clearance. A payment split across two methods has two commitments and so
  two clearances.

  Case to check: The Clearance Manager decides each clearance in a request
  separately, so one half of a split payment can clear while the other is
  rejected, though C-13 says the payment fails unless every method succeeds.
  The source's own sample request also carries two different clearances
  under one clearance ID and accepts both.

  Open point: The Clearance Manager checks for a repeated order and payment
  pair only when the clearance data is not signed by both parties; for
  signed data it checks only the clearance ID. The business must decide
  whether two signed clearances with different IDs for one payment are
  allowed.

\begin{definition}[Clearance status (C-152)]
  \label{def:accounting-clearance-status}
  \uses{def:accounting-clearance-request}
  \notready
  A submitted clearance is Accepted or Rejected, and a rejected one is not
  recorded. An accepted clearance is either Cancelled by the ORA or settled:
  Fully Settled at once, or first Not Settled or Partially Settled and later
  Final Settlement. A settled clearance can be Clawed Back, and one the
  Clearance Manager cannot collect is Written Off; EasyPay clearances show
  Funds Collected or Funds Partially Collected.

  Case to check: A clawed-back clearance is included again in a later
  clearing once the Clearance Manager collects the funds, so Claw Back
  cannot be final; a model that treats every settlement status as final
  refuses that move.

  Open point: The code list names the statuses but gives no table of allowed
  moves and no final statuses. The business must decide, for example,
  whether a clawed-back clearance can be settled again once funds are
  collected, and whether Final Settlement can be clawed back.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574456090}{Settlement with Orders, Codeset List};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574554300}{Settlement with Orders, Financial and Accounting Systems};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574718145}{Settlement with Orders, Remittance of Funds};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574915056}{Settlement with Orders, Definitions}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cancelling a clearance (C-153)]
  \label{def:accounting-clearance-cancellation}
  \uses{def:accounting-clearance-status, def:c15-cancel-order, def:refund}
  \notready
  Only the ORA may cancel a clearance, and only one the Clearance Manager
  has accepted. A cancelled clearance is never settled and the seller is
  notified; a refused cancellation leaves the clearance in its current
  status with a reason.

  Case to check: An order is cancelled with a full refund before the
  remittance date. If the ORA cancels the original clearance and also clears
  the refund to the seller, the seller receives a refund for money it never
  remitted.

  Open point: When a clearance can still be cancelled is left to each
  Clearance Manager. The business must decide the cutoff, such as before the
  clearing notice or before the remittance date, and whether cancelling a
  paid order item cancels its clearance or raises a reverse refund
  clearance.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574587124}{Settlement with Orders, Cancellation Overview};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574685428}{Settlement with Orders, Cancellation Sample Messages};

\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Clearing total (C-154)]
  \label{thm:accounting-clearing-total}
  \uses{def:accounting-clearance-status, def:accounting-net-clearance-amount}
  \notready
  The total in a clearing to a settlement payee is the sum of the amounts
  settled for clearances marked Fully Settled, Partially Settled or Final
  Settlement, minus the amounts clawed back, and a separate bank transfer to
  the payee equals that total. In a clearing to a remittance payer, EasyPay
  clearances are listed but left out of the total, since the EasyPay
  provider pays them.

  Case to check: A payee is due 200.00 in the next clearing and has 300.00
  clawed back. The formula gives minus 100.00, which a transfer to the payee
  cannot carry.

  Open point: The source's sentence on clawbacks that exceed what a payee is
  due in the next clearing is unclear, and such a case makes the total
  negative. The business must decide whether the payee then becomes a
  remittance payer or the clawback is carried forward.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574554300}{Settlement with Orders, Financial and Accounting Systems};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574587102}{Settlement with Orders, Clearing Overview};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574718145}{Settlement with Orders, Remittance of Funds};

\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Net position per remittance date (C-155)]
  \label{thm:accounting-net-position}
  \uses{def:accounting-net-clearance-amount, thm:accounting-clearing-total}
  \notready
  For each party and remittance date, the Clearance Manager adds the net
  clearance amounts of clearances where the party is payee and subtracts
  those where it is payer. A positive result makes the party a settlement
  payee for that amount and a negative one a remittance payer, and the
  results across all parties sum to zero.

  Case to check: A seller owes an airline 100.00 by settlement plan and
  50.00 by EasyPay. If both enter the seller's net position, the seller is
  asked to remit 150.00 while the EasyPay provider also pays the 50.00.

  Open point: The netting rule does not say how EasyPay clearances enter it.
  The EasyPay provider pays them, so they are left out of the seller's
  remittance but included in the airline's settlement; the business must
  decide whether the provider counts as a party in the sum.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574259794}{Settlement with Orders, Clearance Manager};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574718145}{Settlement with Orders, Remittance of Funds};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574488802}{Settlement with Orders, New for release 21.3}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Settled and unsettled clearance amounts (C-156)]
  \label{thm:accounting-settled-plus-remaining}
  \uses{def:accounting-clearance-status, thm:accounting-clearing-total, def:accounting-net-clearance-amount}
  \notready
  For each clearance, the amounts settled across all clearings plus the
  amount still unsettled equal its agreed net clearance amount. A write-off
  removes exactly the amount still unsettled.

  Case to check: The sample clearing shows a partially settled clearance
  with net clearance amount 250.00 and 50.00 unsettled, beside a 200.00
  clearance, with a total of 450.00. The total adds up only if 250.00 is the
  amount settled, so the agreed amount was 300.00 and appears nowhere.

  Open point: In a clearing message the net clearance amount field carries
  the amount settled in that clearing, so the agreed amount travels nowhere.
  The business must decide where the agreed amount is kept and whether a
  clawed-back amount counts as unsettled again.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574554300}{Settlement with Orders, Financial and Accounting Systems};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574718145}{Settlement with Orders, Remittance of Funds};

\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Netting on a change (C-157)]
  \label{def:accounting-change-netting}
  \uses{def:c18-change, thm:c16-penalty-net, thm:c04-item-unsplit, def:c05-holds-value}
  \notready
  When a ticketed flight order item is changed, the airline may offset the
  old item's residual value against the new item's fare and taxes, against a
  change penalty, or both; one of the guide's modes lets the penalty use
  fare residual only. The result is a single amount due to the airline, a
  single amount due by the airline, or nothing. Netting never crosses order
  items or orders, and does not apply to non-flight items with an EMD or to
  unticketed flight items.

  Case to check: The old item leaves 50.00 of fare residual and 50.00 of tax
  residual, and the penalty is 100.00. If the penalty may use fare residual
  only, 50.00 of penalty stays due to the airline while 50.00 of tax
  residual is due by it: two amounts where the guide promises one.

  Open point: The guide says the amount due is calculated correctly but
  gives no formula. The business must decide the formula, including how
  forfeited amounts and a penalty larger than the fare residual enter it;
  the answer also settles part of C-18's open point on the sign of the
  difference.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574488643}{EASD guide, Netting};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574783623}{EASD guide, Netting in Ticketing};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844202079}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a change to an Order containing a paid OrderItem}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Order of accounting postings (C-158)]
  \label{thm:accounting-accounting-in-sequence}
  \uses{thm:c21-version, thm:accounting-accounting-after-payment}
  \notready
  Accounting posts an order's notifications in the sequence in which the
  order changed, with no gaps; a missed notification is recovered before any
  later one is posted. Each posting uses the values the order held at that
  change, and accounting never corrects or recalculates them outside the
  order.

  Case to check: The cancellation notification arrives before the creation
  notification. Posting it first records a refund against an order that has
  no receivable.

  Open point: The source requires chronological posting and a recovery
  process but does not say what fixes the sequence. The business must decide
  whether the order version (C-21), a timestamp or a per-order counter
  orders the postings, and whether a later notification waits for a missing
  one.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/617185366}{Order Accounting, Principles for Finance with Offer and Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/617185338}{Order Accounting, De-specializing Financial Processes};
  \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Commission from offer to order (C-159)]
  \label{thm:accounting-commission-offer-to-order}
  \uses{def:accept, thm:c02-item-per-offer-item, def:accounting-commission-structure}
  \notready
  The commission the airline disclosed on an offer item is the commission
  recorded on the order item created from it. A commission stated once for a
  whole shopping response applies to every offer in that response.

  Case to check: An aggregator merges a response carrying a 5\% commission
  at response level with offers from a second airline. The merged offers
  then seem to carry the first airline's commission.

  Open point: The capability asks the airline to repeat the commission in
  the order confirmation but does not say it must equal what was shown when
  shopping. The business must decide whether commission may change at
  pricing or order creation, and what the seller is owed if the order shows
  less.

  Sources: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/PAYCOM/}{IATA capability PAYCOM};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849050}{EASD guide, Commission and Margin}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Requesting a clearance (C-160)]
  \label{def:accounting-clearance-request}
  \uses{def:instant, def:payment}
  \notready
  Only the ORA sends clearance requests, and it must be the payer or the
  payee of each clearance it sends. It sends the request no later than the
  day after payment commitment, the moment the payment became successful.
  The Clearance Manager rejects an EasyPay clearance whose commitment is
  more than 48 hours older than the request.

  Case to check: A payment committed at 23:55 on day 1 and requested at
  23:50 on day 3 breaks the next-day rule yet passes the 48-hour check.

  Open point: The next-day limit applies to all payment methods, but the
  only enforced limit is 48 hours and only for EasyPay. The business must
  decide what happens to a late settlement plan clearance and whether the
  limit is a calendar day or a duration.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574653069}{Settlement with Orders, Clearance Overview};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574259794}{Settlement with Orders, Clearance Manager};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574456090}{Settlement with Orders, Codeset List};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574816490}{Settlement with Orders, Roles};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574653091}{Settlement with Orders, Enhanced and Simplified Distribution}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Allocating a short remittance (C-161)]
  \label{def:accounting-short-remittance-allocation}
  \uses{thm:accounting-settled-plus-remaining, def:accounting-clearance-status}
  \notready
  When a remittance payer pays short and the Clearance Manager has no credit
  line, the funds are split across the settlement payees in proportion to
  what each is owed. Within each payee, clearances are settled oldest first;
  the first one not fully covered is partially settled and the rest are Not
  Settled.

  Case to check: A payer owes airline A 100.00 (older) and airline B 100.00
  (newer) and pays 100.00. The proportional rule gives each 50.00; the
  oldest-first rule gives A 100.00 and B nothing.

  Open point: Another page says the Clearance Manager applies any payment to
  the payer's outstanding obligations in the order they were recorded,
  whatever the payee. The business must pick one allocation.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574718145}{Settlement with Orders, Remittance of Funds};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SWO/pages/574488849}{Settlement with Orders, Transfer of Funds}.
\end{definition}
