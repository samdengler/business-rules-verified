\begin{definition}[Seller follow-up action (C-113)]
  \label{def:airline-change-follow-up-action}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.FollowUpAction, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.FollowUp, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.Inaction, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.ChangeNotice}
  \uses{def:order, def:instant}
  A change notice carries zero or more seller follow-up actions drawn from
  Accept, Reshop, Cancel, ContactAirline and RepriceAndAccept. Each may
  carry a time limit, a consequence of inaction, and an airline contact
  link. A notice with no follow-up action needs only acknowledgement.  The
  model leaves out the contact link.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455903}{EASD guide, Notification of Airline Initiated Change to an Order}.

  Capabilities: ORDOCN, ORDOC2.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Answering a change notice]
  \label{def:airline-change-answering}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.resolve, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.Resolution, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.SellerAct, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.ChangeNotice.answeredBy, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.ChangeNotice.offers, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.FollowUpReading.records}
  \uses{def:airline-change-follow-up-action, def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  A notice with no follow-up action needs only acknowledgement.  A notice
  with follow-up actions stays pending until the seller takes one of the
  offered actions or a consequence of inaction applies.  The first act of the
  seller that the order records, taken before any consequence applies,
  answers the notice, and answering one follow-up action resolves them all.
  Whether contacting the airline, which happens outside the order, counts as
  an answer is a reading of the sources.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455903}{EASD guide, Notification of Airline Initiated Change to an Order}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[A notice without follow-up actions needs only acknowledgement]
  \label{thm:airline-change-acknowledge-only}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.resolve_acknowledge_only}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  A change notice with no follow-up action asks the seller only to
  acknowledge it.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  Unfold the rule.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An offered action answers the notice]
  \label{thm:airline-change-offered-action-answers}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.resolve_answers}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  When the seller takes an offered Accept, Reshop, Cancel or
  RepriceAndAccept action before any consequence of inaction applies, the
  notice is resolved with one of the seller's acts, under every reading.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  The order records the act, so the search for the first answering act finds
  one.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Answers are offered actions taken in time]
  \label{thm:airline-change-answers-sound}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.resolve_answered_sound}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  A notice is resolved with an answer only when the seller took that action,
  the notice offers it, and the seller took it before any consequence of
  inaction applied.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  The answer is an act that passed the rule's test for answering the notice.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unanswered notices stay pending until they lapse]
  \label{thm:airline-change-unanswered}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.resolve_unanswered}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering, def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  While no act of the seller answers a notice that offers follow-up actions,
  the notice is pending until a consequence of inaction applies, and is then
  resolved with that consequence.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  With no answering act, the rule returns the consequence that applies, or
  pending when none does.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pending follow-up actions (C-108)]
  \label{thm:airline-change-pending-actions}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.contact_airline_answers}
  \uses{def:airline-change-follow-up-action, def:airline-change-inaction-consequence, def:airline-change-answering, thm:airline-change-offered-action-answers}
  A follow-up action the seller has not performed appears in every order
  view until it is resolved. Performing any one of the actions offered for a
  change resolves all of them.  The model checks the second sentence for
  ContactAirline; order views are not modelled.

  Case to check: The itinerary example offers Accept, Cancel, Reshop and
  ContactAirline under one time limit with Auto-acceptance. The seller
  phones the airline, nothing in the order records it, and auto-acceptance
  fires.

  Counterexample: the seller phones the airline two days before the time
  limit of the itinerary change notice.  Under a reading where the order does
  not record contacting the airline, Auto-acceptance applies when the limit
  passes (Lean example \texttt{contactThenAutoAccept}).

  Open point: The source says pending actions are shown but not when they
  stop being pending. The business must decide whether one response clears
  the rest, and whether ContactAirline, which happens offline, ever clears a
  pending Accept.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/815202352}{EASD guide, Airline notifies Seller of changes to an Order}.

  Capabilities: ORDOCN.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  Not proven.  The counterexample above shows the statement is false for the
  rule as written.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Recorded contact stops the time limit]
  \label{thm:airline-change-contact-recorded}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.contact_airline_answers_of_recorded}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  Under a reading where the order records contacting the airline, a seller
  who contacts the airline before any consequence of inaction applies is
  never overtaken by one.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-answering}
  The contact answers the notice, so the rule resolves it with an answer.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Consequence of a missed time limit (C-102)]
  \label{def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.consequenceAt, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.FollowUpReading, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.FollowUp.lapse, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.ChangeNotice.lapse, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.earliest}
  \uses{def:airline-change-follow-up-action, def:instant, def:c15-cancel-order, def:refund}
  If a follow-up action carries a time limit and the seller has not acted by
  then, the airline applies the stated consequence. Auto-acceptance keeps
  the changed order and Auto-cancellation cancels it. The time limit and the
  consequence are both optional.  The model applies the consequence of the
  follow-up action whose time limit passes first, and takes what applies
  when no consequence is stated as a reading of the sources.

  Case to check: The concept page pairs Accept with Auto-cancellation. A
  seller who misses the deadline then costs the customer the whole trip
  after a change the airline made.

  Open point: The business must decide what applies when either the time
  limit or the consequence is missing (the Cancel example has neither), and
  what Auto-cancellation cancels and refunds. It must also decide whether
  the time limit has to fall before the affected flight departs.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/815202352}{EASD guide, Airline notifies Seller of changes to an Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/672825400}{EASD guide, Inform of Impacted Dependent Services}.

  Capabilities: ORDOCN.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The earliest time limit decides the consequence]
  \label{thm:airline-change-earliest-limit}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.lapse_applies_earliest}
  \uses{def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  Once a time limit that carries a consequence of inaction has passed, a
  consequence applies: that of a follow-up action whose time limit is the
  earliest.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  The notice lapses at the earliest of its follow-up actions' lapses, which
  is no later than the one that has passed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No time limit, no consequence]
  \label{thm:airline-change-no-limit}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.no_limit_no_consequence}
  \uses{def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  A notice none of whose follow-up actions has a time limit never has a
  consequence of inaction applied, under every reading.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  A follow-up action with no time limit never lapses.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No consequence before the time limits]
  \label{thm:airline-change-before-limits}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.before_limits_no_consequence}
  \uses{def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  Before every time limit of a notice, no consequence of inaction applies.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  The notice lapses at one of its time limits, and none has passed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Notices are resolved before departure]
  \label{thm:airline-change-resolved-before-departure}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.resolved_before_departure}
  \uses{def:airline-change-inaction-consequence, thm:airline-change-no-limit}
  A notice that offers follow-up actions is resolved before the affected
  flight departs, even if the seller never answers.

  Counterexample: a notice that offers Cancel with neither a time limit nor
  a consequence, like the guide's Cancel example, never lapses.  A notice
  that offers Accept with Auto-acceptance and a time limit a day after
  departure lapses too late.  Both are unresolved when the flight departs,
  under either reading (Lean example \texttt{noticesAtDeparture}).

  Open point: The sources make the time limit and the consequence optional
  and do not tie the time limit to the departure.  The business must decide
  what applies when either is missing, and whether the time limit must fall
  before the affected flight departs.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:airline-change-inaction-consequence}
  Not proven.  The counterexample above shows the statement is false for the
  rule as written.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Voluntary and involuntary change (C-107)]
  \label{def:airline-change-involuntary-change}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.InvoluntaryReading}
  \uses{def:principal, def:order}
  An order change is voluntary when the customer requests it and involuntary
  when the airline imposes it. Resolution 797 commits One Order to support
  servicing, delivery and accounting for both kinds.  The model takes which
  kinds of change are involuntary as a reading of the sources.  A cancelled
  flight and a move to other flights count under every reading, and a change
  the customer made with the airline never does.

  Open point: No source defines the two kinds. Change notices also carry
  changes the customer made directly with the airline, such as a name or
  contact update, so the business must decide which change reasons count as
  involuntary and trigger customer protections.

  Sources: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/672825400}{EASD guide, Inform of Impacted Dependent Services};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDOC2/}{IATA capability ORDOC2};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/815202352}{EASD guide, Airline notifies Seller of changes to an Order}.

  Capabilities: ORDOCN, ORDOC2.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Declining an airline change]
  \label{def:airline-change-decline}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.declineOffer, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.declineQuote, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.Decline, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.DeclineFees, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.DeclineFees.penalty, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.ItemFees, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.DeclineTerms}
  \uses{def:airline-change-follow-up-action, def:airline-change-involuntary-change, def:change-cancellation-price-differential, def:c15-cancel-order, def:c18-change}
  A customer who declines an airline change through Cancel, or whose seller
  lets it lapse into Auto-cancellation, has the affected order items priced
  as a cancellation.  Through Reshop, they are priced as a change to the new
  items.  The penalty is the item's cancellation or change fee.  Whether the
  fees apply when the change is involuntary is a reading of the sources.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574783569}{EASD guide, Cancellation of Order Items}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cancelling a flight without re-accommodation]
  \label{def:airline-change-cancel-flight}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.cancelFlight, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.FlightRefund, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.FlightCancellation, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.cancelOnFlight, Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.flies}
  \uses{def:delivery-end-from-area, def:c06-item-status, def:c08-delivery-status, def:delivery-final-statuses, def:refund}
  When the airline cancels a flight and does not move the customer to
  another one, every live service on that flight, in every order item,
  becomes \texttt{CANCELLED} with delivery status \texttt{UNABLE TO
  DELIVER} where the delivery status table allows the move.  The table has
  no such move from \texttt{REQUESTED}, \texttt{IN PROGRESS} or
  \texttt{SUSPENDED}, so a service in one of those statuses stays live on a
  flight that will not operate.  A service is live when it is neither cancelled nor transferred
  and not in a final delivery status.  The services that depend on the
  flight, such as a bag on it, are services on the same segment.  Order
  items keep their references and prices.  What is refunded for each item
  is a reading of the sources.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/815202352}{EASD guide, Airline notifies Seller of changes to an Order}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Refund for a cancelled flight (C-101)]
  \label{thm:airline-change-refund-undeliverable}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.cancel_flight_refunds}
  \uses{def:c05-holds-value, def:c08-delivery-status, thm:c04-item-unsplit, def:refund, def:ancillaries-flight-change-dependents, def:airline-change-cancel-flight, thm:airline-change-flight-services-cancelled}
  When the airline cancels a flight and does not re-accommodate, the flight
  service and its dependent services become \texttt{CANCELLED} with delivery
  status \texttt{UNABLE TO DELIVER}. The customer is then owed a refund of
  the value of those services.

  Case to check: A two-segment item costs 1000 EUR and the airline cancels

  segment still holds value, and no rule returns any part of the 1000 EUR.

  shows, while its item stays \texttt{ACTIVE} at the full price (Lean

  Open point: The worked example offers no follow-up action and no refund,

  order item, the business must also decide how much of an item's price

  Capabilities: ORDOCN.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Change applied before seller response (C-103)]
  \label{thm:airline-change-applied-before-acceptance}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.AirlineChange.notify_at_notice}
  \uses{def:c06-item-status, thm:c21-version, thm:c19-transfer-balanced, def:airline-change-follow-up-action, def:airline-change-reaccommodation}

  as the itinerary worked example shows.  The version rises, replaced
  services are cancelled or transferred, and the new order item is
  \texttt{ACTIVE} while the seller's Accept is still pending.  The model
  makes this one reading of the sources and proves it for that reading only:
  the order is the order after the change rule for a paid change, one
  version higher.  Whether the original item is kept while Accept is pending
  is the open theorem Original item kept while Accept is pending.

  Case to check: In the itinerary example the original item is already
  \texttt{CANCELLED} at version 2 while Accept is pending. A customer who
  rejects the change cannot get the original flights back, so Accept offers
  no real choice.

  Open point: This is shown only in a worked example. The business must
  decide whether a change awaiting acceptance is provisional, and whether
  declining it restores the original item or only lets the customer cancel
  or reshop from the changed order.

\end{theorem}
