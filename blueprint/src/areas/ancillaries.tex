\section{Ancillaries, seats and bundles}

% Second survey round: candidates written from the survey notes in docs/one-order-survey.md.

\begin{definition}[Dependent service and its parent (C-43)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-service-dependency}
  \uses{def:c01-structure}
  \notready
  A dependent service, such as a bag, seat or meal, belongs to a parent
  flight service for the same passenger on the same passenger segment. The
  order records the link either as a direct reference from the dependent
  service to its parent or only through the shared passenger and segment.

  Case to check: In the seat worked example the seat services carry no
  reference to a flight service. A rule that follows direct references finds
  no dependents of the SEG1 flight service and so reports no impact when
  that flight changes.

  Open point: The business must choose which link defines the dependency,
  and whether one ancillary may depend on another ancillary, such as a
  lounge pass on a premium seat. The impacted dependent services page shows
  a direct service reference, while the add ancillary worked examples link
  bags and seats only to the passenger segment.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/672825400}{EASD guide, Inform of Impacted Dependent Services};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842301531}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order - Service list};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842268814}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order - Seat Availability};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574226524}{EASD guide, Shop for Ancillaries}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Ancillary needs a flight service (C-44)]
  \label{thm:ancillaries-ancillary-needs-flight}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-service-dependency, def:accept, thm:accept-rejects-expired}
  \notready
  A flight-related ancillary enters an order only together with a flight
  service for the same passenger and segment, either in the same acceptance
  or already in the order. A seat map requested for an offer requires the
  offer to hold a flight and be unexpired.

  Open point: The source states this as a purchase precondition and never
  defines flight related. The taxonomy tree gives one answer: codes under
  Flight (0064) are flight related and codes under Journey (319C), such as
  insurance, are not. The business must confirm that and say whether the
  rule holds for the life of the order or only at purchase.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574226524}{EASD guide, Shop for Ancillaries};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPANC/}{IATA capability SHPANC};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPSTO/}{IATA capability SHPSTO};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDRE2/}{IATA capability ORDRE2};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842301482}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652513}{EASD guide, Airline Taxonomy Codeset}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Cancelling a flight with ancillaries (C-45)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-flight-cancel-dependents}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-service-dependency, def:c15-cancel-order, def:c06-item-status, def:c08-delivery-status, def:refund}
  \notready
  When a flight service is cancelled, each service that depends on it ends
  up cancelled, kept, or flagged for the customer. The source shows a seller
  naming the flight item and the ancillary item together in one cancellation
  and gives no rule for a flight cancelled alone.

  Case to check: In the add bag worked example the extra bag item says
  cancellation is not allowed. Cancelling the flight then either cancels the
  bag against its own condition or leaves a confirmed bag on a segment the
  passenger no longer flies. Included bags held as separate zero-priced
  items, which the ARMI guidance permits, face the same choice.

  Open point: The business must decide whether cancelling a flight cancels
  its dependents automatically, is refused until the seller names them too,
  or leaves them in place. It must also decide whose conditions govern the
  refund of each cancelled dependent: the dependent's own or the flight's.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574783569}{EASD guide, Cancellation of Order Items};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842301531}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order - Service list};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980}{ARMI, General Guidance};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/672825400}{EASD guide, Inform of Impacted Dependent Services}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dependents when a flight changes (C-46)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-flight-change-dependents}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-service-dependency, def:c18-change, thm:c19-transfer-balanced, thm:c20-value-store, thm:ancillaries-seat-one-passenger}
  \notready
  When a flight is replaced, the system decides for each dependent service
  whether it moves to the new flight, is cancelled with its value refunded
  or stored, or must be reshopped. The source requires the effect to be
  stated and does not say which effect applies.

  Case to check: Moving paid seat 12C from SEG1 to a replacement flight
  where another passenger holds 12C breaks one passenger per seat. Moving it
  to a different seat may land on a seat with a different price point.

  Open point: The business must give the effect for each service type. Seats
  are the hard case, because the seat number may not exist or may already be
  taken on the new flight.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/672825400}{EASD guide, Inform of Impacted Dependent Services};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574914614}{EASD guide, Shop For Replacement Services};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDRE2/}{IATA capability ORDRE2};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/815202352}{EASD guide, Airline notifies Seller of changes to an Order}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dependent impact shown before acceptance (C-47)]
  \label{thm:ancillaries-dependent-impact-disclosed}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-service-dependency, def:ancillaries-flight-change-dependents}
  \notready
  For a customer-initiated change, every reshop offer that would affect a
  dependent service says so and states the effect. The customer either
  accepts or reshops with the affected service included, so no dependent
  service changes without a stated effect the customer accepted.

  Open point: The source does not define when a dependent service counts as
  impacted or what the stated effect must contain. The business must decide
  whether a change of price, seat number or delivery status counts. It must
  also decide whether the seller must pass the disclosure to the customer
  before acceptance, since the source says only that it should.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/672825400}{EASD guide, Inform of Impacted Dependent Services}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Dependent service lost by airline change (C-48)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-involuntary-dependent-loss}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-service-dependency, def:ancillaries-flight-change-dependents, def:refund, def:c08-delivery-status}
  \notready
  When an airline-initiated change affects a dependent service, the airline
  notifies the seller with any follow-up actions, a time limit and the
  consequence of no action. The customer accepts the change, or declines and
  cancels or reshops, and the reshop may cost more.

  Case to check: A customer paid 50.00 for seat 12C. The airline swaps the
  aircraft and 12C no longer exists. With no automatic refund and
  Auto-acceptance as the consequence of inaction, a customer who does
  nothing loses the 50.00, and one who reshops may pay again for a seat.

  Open point: The business must decide what the customer is owed when an
  airline change removes a paid ancillary: an automatic refund, a free
  replacement, or a reshop at the customer's cost. The source allows extra
  payment on reshop and sets no limit.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/672825400}{EASD guide, Inform of Impacted Dependent Services};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849094}{EASD guide, Seller Follow-up Actions}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[One passenger per seat (C-49)]
  \label{thm:ancillaries-seat-one-passenger}
  \uses{def:c01-structure, def:passenger}
  \notready
  On a given flight, no seat is assigned to two passengers at once. A seat
  assignment holds a seat and the passenger segment it applies to, and the
  seat map marks each seat available or occupied.

  Case to check: The seat worked example selects 12B for PAX01 and 12C for
  PAX02. Nothing in the described rules refuses a request selecting 12B for
  both on SEG1. A segment flown on two legs with a change of aircraft has
  two seat maps, so one seat per segment is ambiguous.

  Open point: The source states no uniqueness rule. The business must decide
  whether uniqueness is checked within one order or across every order on
  the flight. It must also decide whether the unit is the passenger segment
  or the operating leg, since an assigned seat sits on a leg while
  referencing a segment.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574586953}{EASD guide, Seat Map and Availability};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPSTA/}{IATA capability SHPSTA};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPSTO/}{IATA capability SHPSTO};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842268814}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order - Seat Availability}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[One seat per passenger per segment (C-50)]
  \label{thm:ancillaries-seat-one-per-passenger}
  \uses{thm:ancillaries-seat-one-passenger, def:c01-structure, def:c06-item-status}
  \notready
  A passenger holds at most one active seat assignment on each segment of an
  order.

  Case to check: A seller adds 14A for PAX01 on SEG1 through a second quote
  while 12B is still confirmed. Both seat items are \texttt{ACTIVE}, and the
  passenger holds two seats on one segment.

  Open point: The business must decide whether a passenger may hold extra
  seats, for comfort or for a large instrument, and how such a seat is
  represented. It must also decide whether choosing a new seat cancels the
  old seat item and with what refund.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842268814}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order - Seat Availability};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574586953}{EASD guide, Seat Map and Availability}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Seat needs an eligible price point (C-51)]
  \label{thm:ancillaries-seat-eligible-price-point}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-alacarte-item, thm:ancillaries-seat-one-passenger}
  \notready
  A seat is assigned to a passenger on a segment only through the price
  point offer item that the seat references on the seat map, and only if
  that item lists the passenger and segment as eligible. The seat is
  available when chosen.

  Open point: The source attaches price points to available seats and lists
  eligible passengers on each price point, and never says a selection is
  checked against either. The business must decide whether an available seat
  without a price point can be chosen, and who enforces eligibility such as
  exit row restrictions.

  Sources: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPSTP/}{IATA capability SHPSTP};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455867}{EASD guide, Seat Map and Price Points};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842268814}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order - Seat Availability}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[A-la-carte offer item (C-52)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-alacarte-item}
  \uses{def:offer-item, def:c01-structure, def:money}
  An a-la-carte offer item is always optional and has a unit price, a set of
  eligible passengers and a set of eligible segments or journeys. It holds
  no flight service. Each service ordered from it applies to one passenger
  on one segment.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/600637464}{EASD guide, Offers};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPANC/}{IATA capability SHPANC};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPSTP/}{IATA capability SHPSTP}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Price of an a-la-carte selection (C-53)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-alacarte-price}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-alacarte-item, def:money, thm:accept-price-eq-sum, thm:c02-item-per-offer-item}
  \notready
  Choosing an a-la-carte item for a passenger with quantity q over n
  eligible segments creates one order item per passenger and segment. Each
  order item costs the unit price times q, so the selection costs the unit
  price times q times n.

  Case to check: In the add bag worked example the seller selects a 50.00
  bag with quantity 1 over two journeys, and the order gains two order items
  of 50.00, 100.00 in total. Reading quantity 1 as one charge gives 50.00.
  The example also creates two order items from one offer item, which
  contradicts C-02 as written. Each journey there has one segment, so it
  cannot separate per journey from per segment pricing.

  Open point: The source calls the price a unit price without naming the
  unit. The business must decide whether the unit is per passenger per
  segment, per journey or per order, and how quantity multiplies it.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/600637464}{EASD guide, Offers};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842301531}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order - Service list}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Bundle of services (C-54)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-bundle-meaning}
  \uses{def:offer-item, thm:c04-item-unsplit, def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  \notready
  A bundle is a set of services sold together at one price that the customer
  cannot take apart. Conditions set on the offer item apply to every service
  in it.

  Case to check: Under the ARMI reading a flight item and a mandatory meal
  item form one bundle, yet the cancellation page lets a seller delete the
  meal order item alone as a partial cancellation.

  Open point: The EASD bundle page places a bundle inside one offer item,
  while the ARMI bundle capability treats it as the mandatory offer items of
  an offer, each with its own price. The business must pick one, because it
  decides whether removing a bundled ancillary later is a change to one item
  or a cancellation of a separate item.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619818}{EASD guide, Bundles};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPBND/}{IATA capability SHPBND};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/600637464}{EASD guide, Offers};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574783569}{EASD guide, Cancellation of Order Items}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Value of one bundled service (C-55)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-bundle-part-value}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-bundle-meaning, def:refund, def:c05-holds-value, def:c18-change}
  \notready
  When one service in a bundle is cancelled, removed or not delivered, the
  system must give it a share of the bundle price to compute any refund,
  stored value or price difference. The source prices a bundle only as a
  whole.

  Case to check: In the seat worked example one 1000.00 order item holds
  both passengers' flights and bags. The cancellation page removes a
  passenger by cancelling that passenger's services inside such an item and
  notes that the rest must be re-priced, with no rule for the removed share.

  Open point: The business must decide how a bundle price splits among its
  services: by stated component prices, by stand-alone prices, or not at
  all, so that removing a part returns nothing.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619818}{EASD guide, Bundles};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574783569}{EASD guide, Cancellation of Order Items};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842268814}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order - Seat Availability}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Pick M of N price (C-56)]
  \label{thm:ancillaries-pick-m-of-n}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-bundle-meaning, def:offer-item}
  \notready
  In a pick M of N offer item, the customer chooses at most M of the N
  listed services. The price is the same whatever the choice.

  Case to check: Under the fixed price reading a customer who picks no
  service still pays the full price of the item.

  Open point: The source gives a maximum and no minimum. The business must
  decide whether choosing fewer than M, or none, still costs the full price,
  and whether unused picks can be claimed after the order exists.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619818}{EASD guide, Bundles}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Free seat creates a zero-priced item (C-57)]
  \label{thm:ancillaries-free-seat-zero-item}
  \uses{def:c12-zero-commitment, def:ancillaries-alacarte-item}
  \notready
  Assigning a free seat creates its own order item priced at zero, as a paid
  seat does. The payment step covers the free seat with a commitment and
  charges nothing.

  Open point: The two pages of the seat worked example disagree: one says
  payment is processed for free and paid seats alike, the other says a free
  seat is only committed. The business must decide which, which ties to
  C-12.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842301482}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842268814}{EASD guide, Customer Adds an Ancillary to Existing Order - Seat Availability};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980}{ARMI, General Guidance}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Pooled baggage allowance (C-58)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-pooled-baggage}
  \uses{def:passenger}
  \notready
  A baggage allowance associated with several passengers together is shared
  among them, and one associated with each passenger separately limits that
  passenger alone. Under a sum reading, any bags that fit the separate
  allowances also fit the pooled one.

  Case to check: Two passengers pool 2 pieces each with a 46 kg total. Under
  the sum reading they may check 4 bags up to 92 kg. Under the stated totals
  reading they may check 2 bags up to 46 kg, less than they had separately.

  Open point: The pooled example gives each passenger two pieces of 23 kg
  with a 46 kg total. The business must decide whether the pooled limit is
  the sum over passengers or the stated totals once, and whether the
  per-piece maximum still applies to each bag.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574423130}{EASD guide, Baggage Ancillary [\texttt{CON\_SHP\_01}]}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Extra baggage weight (C-59)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-extra-baggage-weight}
  \uses{def:ancillaries-alacarte-price}
  \notready
  Buying extra weight as quantity q of a unit-weight bag service yields one
  extra allowance of q times the unit weight. The airline returns it as a
  single extra bag allowance.

  Case to check: A passenger with one 23 kg bag buys 5 kg extra and checks
  one 28 kg bag. The bag is allowed if the extra raises the existing limit
  and refused if the extra is a separate 5 kg piece.

  Open point: The source describes the extra weight as added on top of an
  existing allowance, but its example returns a separate one-piece allowance
  with its own maximum. The business must decide whether extra weight raises
  an existing bag's limit or adds a separate piece.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574423130}{EASD guide, Baggage Ancillary [\texttt{CON\_SHP\_01}]}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Matching a taxonomy criterion (C-60)]
  \label{def:ancillaries-taxonomy-criteria}
  \uses{def:offer}
  \notready
  Taxonomy codes form a tree rooted at code 0000, and a service tagged with
  a code is also described by every ancestor of that code. A seller's
  service criterion is Required, Exclude or Preferred, and a criterion with
  no level counts as Preferred.

  Case to check: A seller excludes Checked Baggage (13EC). An offer with a
  service tagged Bag (1450), a child of 13EC, passes an exact-code filter
  and fails a tree filter.

  Open point: The source does not say whether a criterion matches
  descendants of its code. It also does not say whether Required and Exclude
  bind the airline, since both capability pages say the airline may consider
  them and taxonomy use is not mandated. The business must settle both
  before any filtering guarantee can hold.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652545}{EASD guide, Airline Taxonomy};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652513}{EASD guide, Airline Taxonomy Codeset};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574816319}{EASD guide, Use of Taxonomy};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPSRV/}{IATA capability SHPSRV};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574914671}{EASD guide, Offer and Order Criteria}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Removing a passenger and dependents (C-61)]
  \label{thm:ancillaries-remove-passenger}
  \uses{def:c01-structure, def:c15-cancel-order, def:ancillaries-pooled-baggage, def:ancillaries-service-dependency}
  \notready
  Removing a passenger from an order cancels every service of that
  passenger, ancillaries included, and keeps every service of the other
  passengers.

  Case to check: Removing adult PAX-1 leaves an infant that references PAX-1
  with no accompanying adult, and leaves PAX-2 holding a pooled allowance
  sized for two.

  Open point: The source is silent on what depends on the removed passenger
  through another passenger. The business must decide what happens to an
  infant on lap whose record references the removed adult, and to a baggage
  allowance pooled with the removed passenger.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574783569}{EASD guide, Cancellation of Order Items};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980}{ARMI, General Guidance};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574423130}{EASD guide, Baggage Ancillary [\texttt{CON\_SHP\_01}]}.
\end{theorem}
