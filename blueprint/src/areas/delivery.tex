\section{Delivery and fulfilment}

\begin{definition}[Delivery status (C-08)]
  \label{def:c08-delivery-status}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.DeliveryStatus}
  \uses{def:c01-structure, def:delivery-state}
  The thirteen delivery statuses of a service.  A service is
  \texttt{CONFIRMED} while the airline waits for payment, becomes
  \texttt{READY TO PROCEED} once paid, and ends \texttt{DELIVERED} or in
  another final status.  This replaces the four delivery states in the pilot
  model.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDSTS/}{IATA capability ORDSTS}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Moves between delivery statuses]
  \label{def:delivery-moves}
  \uses{def:c08-delivery-status, def:step}
  \notready
  The moves a service may make between delivery statuses, read from the
  status definitions, the ORDSTS guidance and the worked examples.
  \texttt{REQUESTED} goes to \texttt{CONFIRMED}; \texttt{CONFIRMED} goes to
  \texttt{READY TO PROCEED} on payment; \texttt{READY TO PROCEED} goes to
  \texttt{READY TO DELIVER}, \texttt{IN PROGRESS} or \texttt{DELIVERED};
  \texttt{READY TO DELIVER} goes to \texttt{IN PROGRESS} or
  \texttt{DELIVERED}; \texttt{IN PROGRESS} goes to \texttt{DELIVERED} or
  \texttt{FAILED TO DELIVER}.  A service before delivery can be cancelled to
  \texttt{REMOVED} or \texttt{REMOVED HOLD VALUE}, or end \texttt{EXPIRED},
  \texttt{NOT CLAIMED}, \texttt{UNABLE TO DELIVER} or
  \texttt{FAILED TO DELIVER}.  \texttt{REMOVED HOLD VALUE} goes only to
  \texttt{REMOVED}.  Any status that is not final can be suspended.  Every
  other move is refused.

  Case to check: the Order Closing worked example moves services from
  \texttt{READY TO PROCEED} straight to \texttt{DELIVERED}, so a table that
  forces every intermediate status in sequence rejects a published example.

  Open point: the sources show only some of these moves; the rest are
  inferred.  The business must confirm the table, decide how a declined
  \texttt{REQUESTED} service ends, and whether an \texttt{IN PROGRESS}
  service can be cancelled or end \texttt{NOT CLAIMED}.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDSTS/}{IATA capability ORDSTS};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/2531491864}{Order Accounting, Order Closing Example}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Suspended services (C-09)]
  \label{thm:c09-suspended}
  \uses{def:c08-delivery-status, def:delivery-moves}
  A suspended service can only return to the status it had before it was
  suspended, or be removed.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Refunds while delivery is in progress (C-10)]
  \label{def:c10-in-progress}
  \uses{def:c08-delivery-status, def:accept-refund}
  \notready
  A service whose delivery is \texttt{IN PROGRESS} may block refunds and other
  financial actions.

  Open point: the source leaves this to each airline.  The business decides
  whether these rules block them.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Who sets each delivery status (C-114)]
  \label{def:delivery-who-sets-status}
  \uses{def:principal, def:c08-delivery-status, def:delivery-moves, thm:c09-suspended}
  \notready
  The ORA's order management system holds the single record of each
  service's delivery status. The airline sets \texttt{REQUESTED},
  \texttt{CONFIRMED}, \texttt{READY TO PROCEED}, \texttt{SUSPENDED},
  \texttt{REMOVED}, \texttt{REMOVED HOLD VALUE} and \texttt{EXPIRED}; the
  delivery provider sets \texttt{READY TO DELIVER}, \texttt{IN PROGRESS},
  \texttt{DELIVERED}, \texttt{UNABLE TO DELIVER}, \texttt{FAILED TO DELIVER}
  and \texttt{NOT CLAIMED} and must report every change to the ORA.

  Case to check: A lounge provider reports \texttt{DELIVERED} for a service
  the airline has \texttt{SUSPENDED} for suspected fraud; if the OMS records
  every report as received, the suspension is lost.

  Open point: The split comes from status definitions, and \texttt{FAILED TO
  DELIVER} names either party. The business must decide whether the OMS
  rejects a provider report that breaks the transition table, and whether
  the airline may overwrite a status the provider set.

  Sources: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652427/Messages}{EASD guide, Messages};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDSTS/}{IATA capability ORDSTS};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/AIDM/pages/607420437/View+IATA+Codeset+Directory}{IATA code set directory}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Payment before delivery starts (C-115)]
  \label{thm:delivery-paid-before-delivery}
  \uses{def:c08-delivery-status, def:payment, def:c12-zero-commitment, def:delivery-moves}
  \notready
  A service reaches \texttt{READY TO DELIVER}, \texttt{IN PROGRESS} or
  \texttt{DELIVERED} only after it has been \texttt{READY TO PROCEED}.
  \texttt{READY TO PROCEED} means the airline has been paid for the service
  and the customer is entitled to it.

  Case to check: In the deferred-payment change example, transferred flight
  services stay \texttt{READY TO PROCEED} inside a new order item that is
  unpaid and carries a payment time limit, so the flight could be delivered
  while the item's balance is outstanding.

  Open point: The sources treat \texttt{READY TO PROCEED} as the payment
  gate and say a delivery notice to the provider does not by itself allow
  delivery to start, but they never forbid delivery from \texttt{CONFIRMED}.
  The business must make that rule and say what counts as paid for deposits,
  zero-amount commitments (C-12) and credit terms.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/AIDM/pages/607420437/View+IATA+Codeset+Directory}{IATA code set directory};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652427/Messages}{EASD guide, Messages};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDDEL/}{IATA capability ORDDEL};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844202130}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a change of a paid order prior to travel resulting in additional collection and a refund}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Final delivery statuses (C-116)]
  \label{def:delivery-final-statuses}
  \uses{def:c08-delivery-status, thm:delivered-not-cancellable, def:delivery-moves}
  \notready
  \texttt{DELIVERED}, \texttt{REMOVED}, \texttt{EXPIRED}, \texttt{NOT
  CLAIMED} and \texttt{FAILED TO DELIVER} are final: no event moves a
  service out of them. Reaching one is the event the OMS reports to
  accounting.

  Case to check: Delivered services are still \texttt{TRANSFERRED} to new
  order items during a change, so finality can only constrain the delivery
  status; the service status and the containing item still change.

  Open point: The accounting message names these five and leaves out
  \texttt{UNABLE TO DELIVER}, and the ORDSTS guidance only says the final
  status depends on the use case. The business must decide whether
  \texttt{UNABLE TO DELIVER} is final or can return to \texttt{READY TO
  PROCEED} once the legal or force majeure constraint lifts.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652427/Messages}{EASD guide, Messages};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDSTS/}{IATA capability ORDSTS};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/2533031940}{EASD guide, 25.4 Release Notes}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Held value after cancellation (C-117)]
  \label{def:delivery-removed-hold-value}
  \uses{def:c05-holds-value, thm:c20-value-store, def:c15-cancel-order, def:refund, def:delivery-moves}
  \notready
  A cancelled service moves to \texttt{REMOVED HOLD VALUE} while the airline
  keeps its value for reuse, and to \texttt{REMOVED} once that value is
  refunded, moved into a value store item, or used up by a paid change. The
  only move out of \texttt{REMOVED HOLD VALUE} is to \texttt{REMOVED}.

  Case to check: A reusable cancellation leaves the item \texttt{ACTIVE} and
  the order \texttt{OPENED}; if the customer never reuses the value and
  nothing moves the service to \texttt{REMOVED}, the order never closes.

  Open point: The sources give three meanings: value the seller can reuse
  later, value held for further servicing, and value not yet refunded
  because the residual cannot yet be calculated. The business must pick one
  and decide whether held value expires and what status follows.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/1404665857}{EASD guide, 24.1 Release Notes};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/AIDM/pages/607420437/View+IATA+Codeset+Directory}{IATA code set directory};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/817594921}{EASD guide, Customer requests a cancellation to paid Order or OrderItems resulting in a Reusable amount};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844202130}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a change of a paid order prior to travel resulting in additional collection and a refund};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844169268}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a cancellation to paid Order or OrderItems resulting in Value Store}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Expiry of an unclaimed service (C-118)]
  \label{def:delivery-expired-unclaimed}
  \uses{def:instant, def:c05-holds-value, def:c07-order-status, def:c11-payment-time-limit, def:delivery-final-statuses}
  \notready
  A service with a consumption time limit moves to \texttt{EXPIRED} when the
  limit passes before the customer claims it, provided the service reached
  at least \texttt{CONFIRMED}. The OMS may set this automatically when the
  limit passes, and \texttt{EXPIRED} is final.

  Case to check: An unpaid \texttt{CONFIRMED} service whose consumption
  window ends before its payment time limit would become \texttt{EXPIRED}
  and be reported to accounting as final, though no sale was ever posted for
  it.

  Open point: The sources do not say whether an unpaid \texttt{CONFIRMED}
  service expires this way or is removed under its payment time limit,
  whether the limit runs while the service is \texttt{SUSPENDED}, or whether
  \texttt{EXPIRED} holds value. The code set lets an order close with
  \texttt{EXPIRED} services and the guide does not, so the business must
  choose.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/AIDM/pages/607420437/View+IATA+Codeset+Directory}{IATA code set directory};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652427/Messages}{EASD guide, Messages}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Passenger no-show (C-119)]
  \label{def:delivery-no-show}
  \uses{def:c05-holds-value, def:refund, def:delivery-final-statuses, def:delivery-expired-unclaimed}
  \notready
  A service moves to \texttt{NOT CLAIMED} when the passenger's own action or
  inaction, such as a no-show, prevents delivery. \texttt{NOT CLAIMED} is
  final, and its report to accounting lets the airline recognize the
  revenue.

  Case to check: Under the French tax rule in the 19.1 release notes, a
  passenger who does not fly may be owed some taxes back; if \texttt{NOT
  CLAIMED} holds no value, the order can close with that refund unpaid.

  Open point: The sources do not say when \texttt{NOT CLAIMED} is set (at
  departure, or once the passenger can no longer rebook), which status
  applies when \texttt{EXPIRED} also fits, or whether \texttt{NOT CLAIMED}
  holds value. The business must also decide how the No Show stage in cancel
  and change restrictions applies to such a service.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/AIDM/pages/607420437/View+IATA+Codeset+Directory}{IATA code set directory};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652427/Messages}{EASD guide, Messages};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259692/Offer+Restrictions}{EASD guide, Offer Restrictions};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574488757}{EASD guide, 19.1 Release Notes}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Refund after a failed delivery (C-120)]
  \label{def:delivery-failed-delivery-owed}
  \uses{def:c05-holds-value, def:refund, def:delivery-final-statuses}
  \notready
  \texttt{FAILED TO DELIVER} records a delivery the airline or its provider
  could not perform because of its own limits. \texttt{UNABLE TO DELIVER}
  records a delivery the provider cannot perform because of legal,
  regulatory or force majeure constraints. The sources set no rule on what
  the customer is owed after either.

  Case to check: If both statuses hold no value, an order whose paid flight
  the airline failed to deliver can close with the customer's money kept.

  Open point: The business must decide whether each status holds value,
  whether a refund, value store or re-accommodation follows, and whether the
  airline-fault case owes more than the force majeure case.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/AIDM/pages/607420437/View+IATA+Codeset+Directory}{IATA code set directory};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/2533031940}{EASD guide, 25.4 Release Notes}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Seller told of status changes (C-121)]
  \label{thm:delivery-seller-told}
  \uses{def:c08-delivery-status, def:delivery-who-sets-status, def:principal}
  \notready
  When a service's delivery status changes, the airline sends the seller an
  order change notification carrying the new status, the seller acknowledges
  it, and the seller tells the customer.

  Case to check: If payment time limit expiry is shown only on retrieval, a
  seller that never retrieves keeps showing the customer a
  \texttt{CONFIRMED} service that no longer exists.

  Open point: ORDST2 asks only that the airline can notify; no source says
  which of the thirteen statuses must be pushed. The business must decide,
  for example, whether \texttt{READY TO DELIVER} and \texttt{IN PROGRESS}
  are pushed, and whether removal at the payment time limit is pushed or
  only shown as error 918 when the seller retrieves the order.

  Sources: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDST2/}{IATA capability ORDST2};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455903}{EASD guide, Notification of Airline Initiated Change to an Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/670203914}{EASD guide, Payment Via Redirection with Airline Notification of Payment Success​};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/815202352}{EASD guide, Airline notifies Seller of changes to an Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/1404665857}{EASD guide, 24.1 Release Notes}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Announced future delivery status (C-122)]
  \label{thm:delivery-future-status-announced}
  \uses{def:c18-change, def:c08-delivery-status, def:delivery-removed-hold-value}
  \notready
  When the airline offers a change or cancellation, it tells the seller the
  future service status and delivery status of every affected service. Once
  the change is accepted and completed, each affected service is in the
  announced status.

  Case to check: In the deferred-payment change example the reshop offer
  announces \texttt{REMOVED}, but after acceptance the services sit in
  \texttt{REMOVED HOLD VALUE} until payment arrives.

  Open point: The field was added so the seller can see the outcome, and no
  source says the outcome must match it or by when. The business must decide
  whether the announced status binds at acceptance or only once any payment
  completes.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/1404665857}{EASD guide, 24.1 Release Notes};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619782/Modification+of+an+Order}{EASD guide, Modification of an Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/817594921}{EASD guide, Customer requests a cancellation to paid Order or OrderItems resulting in a Reusable amount};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844202130}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a change of a paid order prior to travel resulting in additional collection and a refund}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[No changes after closing (C-123)]
  \label{thm:delivery-closed-frozen}
  \uses{def:c07-order-status, def:delivery-final-statuses}
  \notready
  Once an order is \texttt{CLOSED} it accepts no further change, including
  any change to a service's delivery status, though it can still be
  retrieved. In the worked example the order closes when its last services
  are reported \texttt{DELIVERED}.

  Open point: The sources disagree on what closes an order: the guide
  requires services \texttt{DELIVERED} or \texttt{REMOVED}, the code set
  requires items fully paid and services \texttt{DELIVERED}, refunded or
  \texttt{EXPIRED}, and the closing message once named as the only trigger
  was withdrawn in 25.4. The business must pick one trigger for C-07.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652427/Messages}{EASD guide, Messages};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/2531491864}{Order Accounting, Order Closing Example};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/2533031940}{EASD guide, 25.4 Release Notes};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/AIDM/pages/607420437/View+IATA+Codeset+Directory}{IATA code set directory};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Transfer keeps delivery status (C-124)]
  \label{thm:delivery-transfer-keeps-status}
  \uses{thm:c19-transfer-balanced, def:c01-structure, thm:change-cancellation-transferred-original-no-value}
  \notready
  When a service moves to a new order item it keeps its ServiceID and its
  delivery status, and delivered services move too so the order stays
  balanced. The copy left in the original item takes service status
  \texttt{TRANSFERRED}.

  Open point: The guide states that the ServiceID is kept; that the delivery
  status is kept appears only in worked examples. The business must confirm
  that a transfer never changes delivery status.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OA/pages/844202130}{Order Accounting, Customer requests a change of a paid order prior to travel resulting in additional collection and a refund};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/842268712}{EASD guide, Customer requests a change of a paid order prior to travel resulting in additional collection and a value store}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Delivery milestones and status (C-125)]
  \label{thm:delivery-milestones-not-status}
  \uses{def:c08-delivery-status, def:delivery-who-sets-status}
  \notready
  A delivery milestone report, such as a passenger boarded or a bag loaded,
  records progress and does not change the service's delivery status by
  itself. A status change needs its own status report.

  Open point: The source says a milestone does not necessarily change status
  and gives boarding as a case that does not. The business must decide which
  milestone, if any, moves a flight service to \texttt{IN PROGRESS} or
  \texttt{DELIVERED}.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652427/Messages}{EASD guide, Messages}.
\end{theorem}
