\section{Order creation and information}

\begin{theorem}[Order version (C-21)]
  \label{thm:c21-version}
  \uses{def:c18-change, def:order}
  An order's version starts at 1 and goes up by one for each group of changes,
  so the version is always one more than the number of change groups applied.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659/Order+Versioning}{EASD guide, Order Versioning}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Order retention (C-23)]
  \label{def:c23-retention}
  \uses{def:c07-order-status, def:instant}
  An order with an open item stays accessible for at least 13 months from
  creation.  Once all its items reach a final status, it stays accessible for
  at least 7 more days.

  Source: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Selecting items from an offer (C-62)]
  \label{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  \uses{def:offer, def:offer-item, def:passenger, def:accept}
  \notready
  An order is created from a selection of offer items. The selection must
  include every item the offer marks mandatory and name the passengers each
  selected item was built for; optional items may be left out. An item
  without a mandatory mark counts as optional.

  Case to check: An offer holds a flight item and a seat item and neither
  carries the mandatory mark. A seller orders the seat alone, and the order
  holds a seat service with no flight.

  Open point: The guidance says each offer should have at least one
  mandatory item and exempts a la carte offers, so an offer can have none.
  The business must decide which selections are allowed then, such as a seat
  or bag without its flight, and whether an empty selection is rejected.

  Sources: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDCRE/}{IATA capability ORDCRE};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980/General+Guidance}{ARMI, General Guidance};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPANC/}{IATA capability SHPANC};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPBND/}{IATA capability SHPBND};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/600637464}{EASD guide, Offers};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980}{ARMI, General Guidance}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Order items match the selection (C-63)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-items-match-selection}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection, thm:c02-item-per-offer-item, thm:accept-price-eq-sum, def:accept}
  Creating an order yields exactly one order item for each selected offer
  item and none for the items left out. The new order's price is the sum of
  the selected items' prices.

  Case to check: C-02 and the theorem that the order price is the sum of the
  offer's items both range over the whole offer. A seller who leaves out an
  optional bag gets an order without the bag, and both statements as written
  fail.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Atomic order creation (C-64)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-create-atomic}
  \uses{def:accept, def:rejection, thm:accept-rejects-expired, def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  \notready
  A create request either produces one order holding every selected item, or
  produces no order and returns an error. This holds when the selection
  spans several offers.

  Case to check: A selection takes items from offer A, still valid, and
  offer B, expired at the request instant. An airline that creates an order
  from A alone leaves the customer with half of a trip chosen as a whole.

  Open point: The capability pages allow one order from several offers and
  do not say what happens when one of them has expired or fails validation.
  The business must decide whether the whole request fails or the valid
  offers go ahead.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619727/Create+Order+With+Instant+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order With Instant Payment};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Failed payment at order creation (C-65)]
  \label{def:order-creation-instant-payment-failure}
  \uses{thm:order-creation-create-atomic, def:payment, def:c11-payment-time-limit, def:c08-delivery-status}
  \notready
  When an order is created with instant payment and the payment fails, the
  airline either returns the order with a warning or returns an error. The
  guide allows both.

  Case to check: The airline keeps the order and answers with an error. The
  seller concludes nothing was created and sends the request again, so the
  passenger holds two orders on the same flights.

  Open point: The business must decide whether an order survives a failed
  instant payment and, if it does, whether it becomes an unpaid order with a
  payment time limit. It must also decide whether an error response may ever
  leave an order behind.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619727/Create+Order+With+Instant+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order With Instant Payment};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDCRE/}{IATA capability ORDCRE}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[One order per offer (C-66)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-offer-used-once}
  \uses{def:offer, def:accept, thm:order-creation-create-atomic}
  \notready
  An offer produces at most one order. A repeated create request for an
  offer that already produced an order returns the existing order or is
  rejected.

  Case to check: A create request times out at the seller and is sent again
  with a new transaction ID. Nothing in the sources stops the airline
  creating a second order from the same offer.

  Open point: The guide says the airline checks that the offer is valid
  without listing the checks, and its retransmission ID for recovering a
  lost response is not tied to order creation. The business must decide
  whether creating an order uses up the offer and how a repeated request is
  recognized.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574226512/Identifier+Usage}{EASD guide, Identifier Usage};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619693/Payload+Attributes}{EASD guide, Payload Attributes}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Statuses of a new order (C-67)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-new-order-state}
  \uses{def:c05-holds-value, def:c06-item-status, def:c07-order-status, def:c08-delivery-status, def:c11-payment-time-limit}
  \notready
  A new order is \texttt{OPENED} and each of its order items is
  \texttt{ACTIVE}. Each service starts as \texttt{CONFIRMED},
  \texttt{REQUESTED} or \texttt{WAITLISTED}. A confirmed service has
  delivery status \texttt{CONFIRMED} when the order is created without
  payment and \texttt{READY TO PROCEED} when it is paid at creation.

  Case to check: If C-05 rules that delivery status \texttt{CONFIRMED} holds
  no value, a new unpaid item has no service holding value and fails the
  \texttt{ACTIVE} condition of C-06 at the moment it is created.

  Open point: The guide calls \texttt{CONFIRMED} one of the initial service
  statuses without listing the others, and gives no delivery status for a
  requested or waitlisted service. The business must decide those statuses
  and whether such a service gets a payment time limit or can be paid at
  creation.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619727/Create+Order+With+Instant+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order With Instant Payment}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Paying for part of an order (C-68)]
  \label{def:order-creation-pay-some-items}
  \uses{thm:payment-time-limit-on-unpaid, def:payment, thm:c04-item-unsplit, def:c11-payment-time-limit, def:c12-zero-commitment}
  \notready
  A seller can pay for unpaid order items in a later change, so an order can
  be partly paid. Each payment names the order items it pays for, including
  items priced at zero.

  Case to check: A bag item is paid and moves to \texttt{READY TO PROCEED}
  while the flight item stays unpaid. When the flight item's limit passes,
  the order holds a paid bag for a flight the passenger no longer has.

  Open point: The sources do not say whether a seller may pay one item and
  leave unpaid another item it depends on, or what happens to the paid item
  when the other item's time limit passes. The business must also decide
  whether the price of an unpaid item is held until its time limit.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980/General+Guidance}{ARMI, General Guidance}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Order items in a retrieved order (C-69)]
  \label{def:order-creation-retrieved-view-items}
  \uses{def:c01-structure, def:c06-item-status, def:c23-retention}
  \notready
  A retrieved order shows every \texttt{ACTIVE} and \texttt{CLOSED} order
  item. \texttt{CANCELLED} items are left out, except when all items were
  cancelled in one transaction; those are shown for the retention period.
  The response to a change also shows the items that change cancelled.

  Case to check: Items A and B are cancelled in separate transactions. Every
  item is \texttt{CANCELLED} but the exception does not apply, so the
  retrieved order shows no items while Resolution 797 still requires access
  to it and C-01 requires at least one item.

  Open point: The business must decide whether the exception covers every
  order whose items are all cancelled, however many transactions that took.
  It must also decide which retention period applies, since the guide points
  to Resolution 797 without saying whether the 7 day or the 13 month period
  is meant.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Read access to an order (C-70)]
  \label{def:order-creation-read-access}
  \uses{def:principal, def:classification, def:order-creation-order-id, def:order-creation-chain-identity-check}
  \notready
  A retrieve, list or history request returns an order only to a requester
  entitled to it. The sources describe these requests as made by the seller
  and give no rule on which sellers.

  Case to check: If holding the order ID is enough, anyone who reads it from
  a receipt can retrieve the order and its history, since the ID is the
  airline's public codes plus 8 characters.

  Open point: The business must decide who is entitled: the seller that
  created the order, any seller or secondary agent that has acted on it, any
  party in the distribution chain of a past request, or anyone holding the
  order ID or booking reference. It must also decide which orders a search
  across many orders may return.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455891/Retrieve+an+Order}{EASD guide, Retrieve an Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574816364/Historical+Information+on+Orders}{EASD guide, Historical Information on Orders};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652557/Order+History}{EASD guide, Order History};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDLST/}{IATA capability ORDLST};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDHIS/}{IATA capability ORDHIS};
  \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Group order limits (C-71)]
  \label{def:order-creation-group-order}
  \uses{def:order, def:passenger, def:payment}
  \notready
  An order is a group order when the create request names a passenger group.
  Each airline sets the minimum and maximum number of passengers and the
  passenger details required at creation and at payment, and refuses
  creation or payment when these are not met.

  Case to check: A group order is created with 10 passengers against a
  minimum of 10. One passenger cancels, and the sources do not say whether
  the remaining 9 still form a group order.

  Open point: The business must set the thresholds and the required details.
  It must also decide what happens when passengers leave a group order and
  the count falls below the minimum.

  Source: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDGRP/}{IATA capability ORDGRP}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[History rebuilds every version (C-72)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-history-rebuilds-versions}
  \uses{thm:c21-version, def:order, def:c18-change}
  \notready
  An order's history starts with the whole order at version 1, recorded as
  one addition, followed by one change group per later version. Applying the
  groups up to version n to version 1 gives the order as it was at version
  n, and applying all of them gives the order that retrieval returns.

  Case to check: Applied newest first, a group that removes an item in
  version 4 runs before the group that added it in version 3, and the item
  reappears in the rebuilt current order.

  Open point: The guide lists change groups oldest first with each raising
  the version by one, while the ORDHIS capability page lists them newest
  first and says each group lowers the version. The business must fix the
  order, and decide whether a history request from version v includes the
  group that produced v.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652557/Order+History}{EASD guide, Order History};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659/Order+Versioning}{EASD guide, Order Versioning};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574816364/Historical+Information+on+Orders}{EASD guide, Historical Information on Orders};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455891/Retrieve+an+Order}{EASD guide, Retrieve an Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDHIS/}{IATA capability ORDHIS};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574652557}{EASD guide, Order History};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659}{EASD guide, Order Versioning};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDOC2/}{IATA capability ORDOC2}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Version tracks seller-visible changes (C-73)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-version-tracks-view}
  \uses{thm:c21-version, thm:order-creation-history-rebuilds-versions}
  \notready
  The airline raises an order's version whenever an element the seller can
  see changes. Two views of one order with the same version therefore show
  the same seller-visible data.

  Case to check: The airline records a new payment status without raising
  the version. A seller holding version 4 passes the version check and
  changes the order on data that no longer holds.

  Open point: The guide says the airline should raise the version, and it
  lets the airline group many changes into one version. The business must
  decide whether every seller-visible change, including airline-initiated
  ones, must raise the version before the seller next sees the order.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659/Order+Versioning}{EASD guide, Order Versioning};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659}{EASD guide, Order Versioning};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDOC2/}{IATA capability ORDOC2}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Change against a stale version (C-74)]
  \label{def:order-creation-stale-version-change}
  \uses{thm:c21-version, def:c18-change, def:accept, thm:order-creation-version-tracks-view}
  \notready
  A change request may carry the order version the seller believes is
  current. The airline proceeds when it matches and may return an error when
  it does not, and a request without a version proceeds. Offers made while
  servicing an order carry the version they were built on.

  Case to check: Two sellers read version 3. The first change makes version
  4. The second change still carries version 3, and because the guide only
  permits an error, the airline may apply it over a change its seller never
  saw.

  Open point: The business must decide whether a version mismatch always
  rejects the change and whether a change without a version is allowed. It
  must also decide whether an offer built on an older version can still be
  accepted.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659/Order+Versioning}{EASD guide, Order Versioning};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659}{EASD guide, Order Versioning};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDOC2/}{IATA capability ORDOC2}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Service IDs in an order (C-75)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-service-id-unique}
  \uses{def:c01-structure, thm:c03-unique-item-ids, thm:c19-transfer-balanced}
  \notready
  Service IDs are unique within an order and never change. A service moved
  to a new order item in a change keeps its ID.

  Case to check: A change moves service S1 from item A to item B. Item A
  keeps S1 marked \texttt{TRANSFERRED} and item B holds S1, so the order has
  two service records with ID S1.

  Open point: The identifier rules call unique service IDs and unique order
  item IDs a best practice, while the Order page states item IDs are unique,
  which bears on C-03. A transfer leaves the original \texttt{TRANSFERRED}
  service and the moved service in one order with the same ID, so the
  business must decide whether uniqueness covers every service record or
  only those not \texttt{TRANSFERRED}.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574226512/Identifier+Usage}{EASD guide, Identifier Usage};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/815202352}{EASD guide, Airline notifies Seller of changes to an Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDOC2/}{IATA capability ORDOC2}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Retention after first usage (C-76)]
  \label{def:order-creation-retention-first-usage}
  \uses{def:c23-retention, def:c05-holds-value, def:c06-item-status, thm:c20-value-store, def:instant}
  \notready
  Building on C-23, the offer responsible airline also keeps an order with
  an open item accessible for 13 months from its first usage. While an item
  is open, access is required until the later of 13 months from creation and
  13 months from first usage.

  Case to check: An item whose services are \texttt{REMOVED HOLD VALUE}
  stays open with the customer's stored value for two years and is never
  used. After 13 months the resolution no longer requires access, so the
  order holding that value can be purged.

  Open point: Resolution 797 does not define first usage or open item, and
  requires nothing for an order whose item is still open after both 13 month
  periods. The business must define both terms, decide how 13 months is
  counted on UTC instants, and decide whether order history follows the same
  periods.

  Source: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Order ID (C-77)]
  \label{def:order-creation-order-id}
  \uses{def:order}
  \notready
  An order ID joins the owning airline's 2 or 3 character designator, its 3
  character accounting code (XXX when it has none) and an 8 character
  reference unique within that airline. The ID is treated as globally
  unique, never changes, and every later request for the order uses it.

  Case to check: Two airlines that share a controlled duplicate designator
  and both use XXX issue the same order ID whenever their 8 character
  references coincide.

  Open point: Global uniqueness follows only if designator and accounting
  code together name one airline. The business must decide how uniqueness
  holds when two airlines share a designator and neither has an accounting
  code, and how long a booking reference may still be used to retrieve an
  order.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259647/Order+ID}{EASD guide, Order ID};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574226512/Identifier+Usage}{EASD guide, Identifier Usage};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980/General+Guidance}{ARMI, General Guidance}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Order owner is the offer owner (C-78)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-order-owner}
  \uses{def:offer, def:order, def:order-creation-order-id, thm:order-creation-create-atomic}
  \notready
  The airline responsible for the accepted offer owns the resulting order.
  The order's owner code is that airline's designator, which also begins the
  order ID.

  Case to check: A seller selects offer A from airline XB and offer B from
  airline YY in one create request. The resulting order would have two
  owners.

  Open point: Resolution 797 makes each offer responsible airline the owner
  of the orders for its own offers, and the capability pages allow one order
  from several offers. The business must decide whether one order may
  combine offers from different responsible airlines.

  Sources: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259647/Order+ID}{EASD guide, Order ID};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Order total from item prices (C-79)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-order-total-derived}
  \uses{def:order, def:money-add, thm:accept-price-eq-sum, thm:c04-item-unsplit, thm:order-creation-create-atomic}
  \notready
  Prices are held on order items, and a stored total price at order level is
  deprecated. An order's total at any version is the sum of its current item
  prices.

  Case to check: Items from an offer priced in EUR and an offer priced in
  USD go into one order. Adding them fails under def:money-add, so the order
  has no total.

  Open point: The sources do not say whether one order may hold items in
  more than one currency, which can happen when items come from several
  offers. The business must decide, since a mixed-currency total has no
  value under the current money rules.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980/General+Guidance}{ARMI, General Guidance};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Verifying distribution chain identities (C-80)]
  \label{def:order-creation-chain-identity-check}
  \uses{def:principal}
  \notready
  The carrier accepts a participant's identity only when its credential is
  authentic, unexpired and unrevoked, carries the identifier the participant
  uses in the message, is presented by its owner, and is presented for this
  transaction. Every participant except the one connected to the carrier
  sends such a presentation, and intermediaries pass on all they receive.

  Case to check: A seller's credential is revoked at 09:00. An airline that
  refreshes revocation lists once a day accepts that seller's create request
  at 10:00.

  Open point: The guide does not say what the carrier does when a check
  fails, and leaves the refresh rate of revocation lists to each airline.
  The business must decide both.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/815464449/Verification+of+Identities+in+Distribution+Chain}{EASD guide, Verification of Identities in Distribution Chain};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574488619/Distribution+Chain}{EASD guide, Distribution Chain};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980/General+Guidance}{ARMI, General Guidance}.
\end{definition}
