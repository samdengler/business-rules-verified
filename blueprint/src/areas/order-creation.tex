\begin{theorem}[Order version (C-21)]
  \label{thm:c21-version}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.version_counts_groups}
  \uses{def:c18-change, def:order, def:order-creation-record-changes}
  An order's version starts at 1 and goes up by one for each group of changes,
  so the version is always one more than the number of change groups applied.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659/Order+Versioning}{EASD guide, Order Versioning}.

  Capabilities: ORDHIS.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-record-changes}
  By induction on the change groups: each raises the version by one and adds
  one group to the history.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Recording changes to an order]
  \label{def:order-creation-record-changes}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.recordChanges, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.OrderRecord, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.OrderRecord.create, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.OrderRecord.after, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.OrderChange, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.ChangeGroup, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.applyChange, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.applyGroup, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.replay}
  \uses{def:c01-structure}
  The airline keeps an order as created, at version 1, with the change groups
  applied since, oldest first.  A change adds, replaces or removes an order
  item.  Applying a group of changes applies each in turn, records the group
  and raises the version by one.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659/Order+Versioning}{EASD guide, Order Versioning}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[New orders start at version 1]
  \label{thm:order-creation-created-version-one}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.create_version_one}
  \uses{def:order-creation-record-changes, def:order-creation-history}
  A new order is at version 1, with no change groups and an empty history.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659/Order+Versioning}{EASD guide, Order Versioning}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-record-changes}
  Unfold the record of a new order.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Each change group raises the version by one]
  \label{thm:order-creation-version-raised}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.record_raises_version}
  \uses{def:order-creation-record-changes}
  Applying a group of changes applies it to the order, records it in the
  history and raises the version by one.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574259659/Order+Versioning}{EASD guide, Order Versioning}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-record-changes}
  Unfold the rule.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Order retention (C-23)]
  \label{def:c23-retention}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.mustKeep, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.MonthCount, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.sevenDays}
  \uses{def:c07-order-status, def:instant}
  An order with an open item stays accessible for at least 13 months from
  creation.  Once all its items reach a final status, it stays accessible for
  at least 7 more days.  The model reads an open item as an active one, and
  takes how 13 months are counted on UTC instants as a parameter.

  Source: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797}.

  Capabilities: ORDRET.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Orders kept 13 months from creation]
  \label{thm:order-creation-keep-from-creation}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.keep_from_creation}
  \uses{def:c23-retention}
  An order with an active item is kept until 13 months after its creation,
  under every reading of the guide and every count of months.

  Source: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:c23-retention}
  An order with an active item is opened, and the first window applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Orders kept 7 days after their items are final]
  \label{thm:order-creation-keep-after-final}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.keep_after_final}
  \uses{def:c23-retention, thm:one-active-not-closed, thm:one-active-not-cancelled}
  An order whose items have all become closed or cancelled is kept for 7 days
  after that, under every reading of the guide and every count of months.

  Source: \href{https://www.iata.org/contentassets/72cbd60393ff42b5975d90ce9e049a7d/oneorder-resolution-797.pdf}{IATA Resolution 797}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{thm:one-active-not-closed, thm:one-active-not-cancelled}
  A closed or cancelled item is never active, so the order is not opened and
  the 7 day window applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Selecting items from an offer (C-62)]
  \label{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.selectItems, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.MarkedOffer, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.Selection, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.CreateRejection}
  \uses{def:offer, def:offer-item, def:passenger, def:accept, def:one-offer}
  An order is created from a selection of offer items. The selection must
  include every item the offer marks mandatory and name the passengers each
  selected item was built for; optional items may be left out. An item
  without a mandatory mark counts as optional.

  Case to check: An offer holds a flight item and a seat item and neither
  carries the mandatory mark. A seller orders the seat alone, and the order
  holds a seat service with no flight.

  Open point: The guidance says each offer should have at least one
  mandatory item and exempts a la carte offers, so an offer can have none.
  The business must decide which selections are allowed then, such as a seat
  or bag without its flight, and whether an empty selection is rejected.

  Sources: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDCRE/}{IATA capability ORDCRE};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980/General+Guidance}{ARMI, General Guidance};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPANC/}{IATA capability SHPANC};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/SHPBND/}{IATA capability SHPBND};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/600637464}{EASD guide, Offers};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980}{ARMI, General Guidance}.

  Capabilities: ORDCRE, ORDWPM, ORDCWT, SHPANC, SHPBND.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Unknown items are rejected]
  \label{thm:order-creation-select-unknown}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.select_rejects_unknown}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  A selection that names an item the offer does not hold is rejected.

  Source: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  An accepted selection names only items of the offer.
\end{proof}

  A selection that leaves out an item the offer marks mandatory is rejected.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980/General+Guidance}{ARMI, General Guidance}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A selection takes the items it names]
  \label{thm:order-creation-select-named}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.select_takes_named_items}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  An accepted selection takes exactly the offer items whose references it
  names, and every reference it names is an item it takes.

  Source: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  The items taken are the offer's items filtered by the selection, and every
  name was checked against the offer.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every order holds a mandatory item]
  \label{thm:order-creation-select-includes-mandatory}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.select_includes_mandatory}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  Every accepted selection includes at least one item the offer marks
  mandatory.

  Counterexample: an a la carte offer holds a flight and a seat and marks
  neither mandatory.  A seller selects the seat alone, and the selection is
  accepted with no mandatory item and no flight (Lean example
  \texttt{seatWithoutFlight}).

  Open point: the guidance asks for at least one mandatory item in each offer
  and exempts a la carte offers.  The business must decide which selections
  are allowed from an offer that marks no item mandatory, and whether a seat
  or bag may be ordered without its flight.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980/General+Guidance}{ARMI, General Guidance}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  Not proven.  The counterexample above shows the statement is false for the
  rule as written.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Offers with a mandatory item]
  \label{thm:order-creation-select-mandatory-of-marked}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.select_includes_mandatory_of_marked}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection, thm:order-creation-select-mandatory}
  When an offer marks at least one item mandatory, every accepted selection
  includes one.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARMI/pages/584318980/General+Guidance}{ARMI, General Guidance}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection}
  A mandatory item must be named, and every name is an item of the offer.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Order items match the selection (C-63)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-items-match-selection}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.create_items_match_selection}
  \uses{def:order-creation-create-order, def:order-creation-offer-item-selection, thm:c02-item-per-offer-item, thm:accept-price-eq-sum, def:accept}
  Creating an order yields exactly one order item for each selected offer
  item and none for the items left out. The new order's price is the sum of
  the selected items' prices.

  Case to check: C-02 and the theorem that the order price is the sum of the
  offer's items both range over the whole offer. A seller who leaves out an
  optional bag gets an order without the bag, and both statements as written
  fail.

  Counterexample: one create request at 11:30 UTC selects an outbound flight
  at 400.00 EUR from an offer valid until 12:00 UTC and a return flight at
  380.00 EUR from an offer that expired at 11:00 UTC.  Under the reading where
  the offers that pass go ahead, the order holds the outbound flight alone
  and costs 400.00 EUR (Lean example \texttt{oneOfferExpired}).
  The return flight the seller selected has no order item.

  Open point: the business must decide whether a request with a rejected
  offer fails as a whole or goes ahead with the offers that pass (see C-64).

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM}.

  Capabilities: ORDCRE, ORDWPM.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-create-order}
  Not proven.  The counterexample above shows the statement is false for the
  rule as written.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Orders hold the selection when the whole request is checked]
  \label{thm:order-creation-items-match-selection-of-whole}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.create_items_match_selection_of_wholeRequest}
  \uses{def:order-creation-create-order}
  Under the reading that rejects the whole request, an accepted request
  becomes an order with one order item for each item selected from each offer
  and none for the items left out.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574849062/Order}{EASD guide, Order};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-create-order}
  Each selection becomes its selected items, and the order holds the items of
  every selection in turn.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Creating one order from several offers]
  \label{def:order-creation-create-order}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.createOrder, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.MultiOfferHandling, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.pickItems, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.passingItems, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.orderItemsFor, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.orderItemsForAll, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.selectedFrom, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.itemPrefix}
  \uses{def:order-creation-offer-item-selection, def:one-accept, def:rejection}
  A create request holds a selection from each of one or more offers.  Each
  offer must be one the airline holds and not expired, each selection must be
  accepted, and each selected item must be priced in its offer's currency.
  The sources do not say what happens when one selection is rejected, so the
  model takes the answer as a parameter with two readings: the whole request
  is rejected, or the selections that pass go ahead.  One order holds the
  items picked, and a request that picks nothing is rejected.

  Sources: \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDCRE/}{IATA capability ORDCRE};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM}.
\end{definition}

  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.create_rejects_empty}
  \uses{def:order-creation-create-order, def:c01-structure}
  A request that selects no item from any offer is rejected under both

\begin{theorem}[Atomic order creation (C-64)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-create-atomic}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.create_rejects_expired}
  \uses{def:accept, def:rejection, thm:accept-rejects-expired, def:order-creation-offer-item-selection, def:order-creation-create-order}
  A create request either produces one order holding every selected item, or
  produces no order and returns an error. This holds when the selection
  spans several offers.

  Case to check: A selection takes items from offer A, still valid, and
  offer B, expired at the request instant. An airline that creates an order
  from A alone leaves the customer with half of a trip chosen as a whole.

  Counterexample: one create request at 11:30 UTC selects an outbound flight
  at 400.00 EUR from an offer valid until 12:00 UTC and a return flight at
  380.00 EUR from an offer that expired at 11:00 UTC.  Under the reading where
  the offers that pass go ahead, the order holds the outbound flight alone
  and costs 400.00 EUR (Lean example \texttt{oneOfferExpired}).

  Open point: The capability pages allow one order from several offers and
  do not say what happens when one of them has expired or fails validation.
  The business must decide whether the whole request fails or the valid
  offers go ahead.  The model takes the answer as a parameter with two
  readings.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619727/Create+Order+With+Instant+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order With Instant Payment};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDWPM/}{IATA capability ORDWPM}.

  Capabilities: ORDCRE, ORDWPM.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-create-order}
  Not proven.  The counterexample above shows the statement is false for the
  rule as written.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rejecting the whole request]
  \label{thm:order-creation-create-atomic-of-whole}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.create_rejects_expired_of_wholeRequest}
  \uses{def:order-creation-create-order, thm:order-creation-create-atomic}
  Under the reading that rejects the whole request, a request that names an
  offer which has expired at the instant of the request is rejected, and no
  order is created.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-create-order}
  The expired offer's selection is rejected, and a rejected selection rejects
  the whole request.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Failed payment at order creation (C-65)]
  \label{def:order-creation-instant-payment-failure}
  \uses{thm:order-creation-create-atomic, def:payment, def:c11-payment-time-limit, def:c08-delivery-status}
  \notready
  When an order is created with instant payment and the payment fails, the
  airline either returns the order with a warning or returns an error. The
  guide allows both.

  Case to check: The airline keeps the order and answers with an error. The
  seller concludes nothing was created and sends the request again, so the
  passenger holds two orders on the same flights.

  Open point: The business must decide whether an order survives a failed
  instant payment and, if it does, whether it becomes an unpaid order with a
  payment time limit. It must also decide whether an error response may ever
  leave an order behind.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619727/Create+Order+With+Instant+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order With Instant Payment};
  \href{https://retailing.iata.org/armi/docs/ORDCRE/}{IATA capability ORDCRE}.

  Capabilities: ORDCRE.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[One order per offer (C-66)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-offer-used-once}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.once_per_offer}
  \uses{def:offer, def:accept, thm:order-creation-create-atomic, def:order-creation-repeat}
  An offer produces at most one order. A repeated create request for an
  offer that already produced an order returns the existing order or is
  rejected.

  Case to check: A create request times out at the seller and is sent again
  with a new transaction ID. Nothing in the sources stops the airline
  creating a second order from the same offer.

  Counterexample: a seller sends a create request for the trip offer's
  flight and gets order ORD-5.  The request is sent again after a timeout.
  Under the reading where the airline creates another order, the seller gets
  ORD-6 and the trip offer has produced two orders (Lean example
  \texttt{repeatedRequest}).

  Open point: The guide says the airline checks that the offer is valid
  without listing the checks, and its retransmission ID for recovering a
  lost response is not tied to order creation. The business must decide
  whether creating an order uses up the offer and how a repeated request is
  recognized.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574226512/Identifier+Usage}{EASD guide, Identifier Usage};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574619693/Payload+Attributes}{EASD guide, Payload Attributes}.

  Capabilities: ORDCRE, ORDWPM.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-repeat}
  Not proven.  The counterexample above shows the statement is false for the
  rule as written.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Repeated create requests]
  \label{def:order-creation-repeat}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.createOnce, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.CreatedOrder, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.CreatedOrder.fromAny, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.RepeatHandling, Rules.OneOrder.Creation.OncePerOffer}
  \uses{def:order-creation-create-order}
  The airline records each order it creates with the offers it came from.  A
  request that names an offer with an order is a repeated request.  The
  sources are silent on what the airline does with it, so the model takes the
  answer as a parameter with three readings: return the existing order,
  reject the request, or create another order.

  Sources: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment};
  \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574226512/Identifier+Usage}{EASD guide, Identifier Usage}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[New orders are recorded against their offers]
  \label{thm:order-creation-records-offers}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.create_once_records}
  \uses{def:order-creation-repeat}
  A request that names no offer with an order is created as a new order and
  recorded against every offer it names, under every reading.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-repeat}
  No recorded order matches the request, so the order is created and
  recorded.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Repeated requests get the existing order]
  \label{thm:order-creation-repeat-existing}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.repeat_returns_existing}
  \uses{def:order-creation-repeat}
  Under the reading that returns the existing order, a repeated request is
  answered with an order recorded for one of the offers it names, and nothing
  new is recorded.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-repeat}
  The first recorded order that matches the request is returned.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Repeated requests can be rejected]
  \label{thm:order-creation-repeat-rejected}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.repeat_rejected}
  \uses{def:order-creation-repeat}
  Under the reading that rejects repeated requests, a request that names an
  offer with an order is rejected.

  Source: \href{https://standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EASD/pages/574455879/Create+Order+Without+Payment}{EASD guide, Create Order Without Payment}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:order-creation-repeat}

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Returning or rejecting repeats keeps one order per offer]
  \label{thm:order-creation-once-per-offer-of-ne}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.once_per_offer_of_ne}
  \uses{def:order-creation-repeat, thm:order-creation-records-offers}
  Under the readings that return the existing order or reject the request, no
  offer ever appears in two recorded orders.

\begin{theorem}[Statuses of a new order (C-67)]

\begin{theorem}[History rebuilds every version (C-72)]
  \label{thm:order-creation-history-rebuilds-versions}
  \lean{Rules.OneOrder.Creation.history_rebuilds_current}
  \uses{thm:c21-version, def:order, def:c18-change, def:order-creation-history}
  An order's history starts with the whole order at version 1, recorded as
  one addition, followed by one change group per later version. Applying the
  groups up to version n to version 1 gives the order as it was at version
  n, and applying all of them gives the order that retrieval returns.

  Case to check: Applied newest first, a group that removes an item in
  version 4 runs before the group that added it in version 3, and the item
  reappears in the rebuilt current order.

  Counterexample: the airline adds a bag to an order at version 2 and removes
  it at version 3.  Listed newest first and applied in that order, the removal
  runs before the bag was added, and the rebuilt order holds the bag that the
  current order does not (Lean example \texttt{newestFirstHistory}).

  Open point: The guide lists change groups oldest first with each raising
  the version by one, while the ORDHIS capability page lists them newest
  first and says each group lowers the version. The business must fix the
  order, and decide whether a history request from version v includes the
  group that produced v.

  uses in the message, is presented by its owner, and is presented for this
  transaction. Every participant except the one connected to the carrier
  sends such a presentation, and intermediaries pass on all they receive.

  Case to check: A seller's credential is revoked at 09:00. An airline that
  refreshes revocation lists once a day accepts that seller's create request
  at 10:00.

  Open point: The guide does not say what the carrier does when a check
  fails, and leaves the refresh rate of revocation lists to each airline.
  The business must decide both.
